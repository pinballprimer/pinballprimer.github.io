Darts (Williams, 1960)

Game rules

The main goal of Darts is to completely light the dartboards on the backglass of the game. There are three such dartboards, labelled A, B, and C. To complete a dartboard, you must light its letter by draining down the corresponding center out lane, as well as collect the 8 numbers on that dartboard from around the playfield. 1, 2, and 3 are collected at top saucers that score 50,000 points. 4, 6, and 8 are collected at upper left standup targets, and 5, 7, and 9 are at upper right standup targets, which all score 10,000 points. 10 and 11 are earned at the middle side lanes, which score 100,000. A, B, and C are earned at the three center out lanes between the flippers, and also score 100,000 each. Dartboard A requires all numbers except 8, 10, and 11; dartboard B requires all numbers except 2, 9, and 11; dartboard C requires all numbers except 1, 9, and 10. This means that 9, 10, and 11 appear on just one dartboard each, but 3, 4, 5, 6, and 7 each appear on all three.
The gobble hole in the center of the playfield scores 500,000 points, plus one Special (always a free game) for each completed dartboard, and ends the current ball in play. Once a letter or number is lit, it remains lit for the rest of the game.
It is extremely important to get 1, 2, and 3 on plunges whenever possible, as it is very difficult to get back to the top center of the playfield. Nudging off the bumpers to get more than one top saucer in one trip to the top of the playfield can be very helpful. The 6, 7, 8, and 9 targets can usually be shot directly, but 4 and 5 likely require further nudging or pop bumper luck.

The green bumpers labelled Jet always score 10,000 points. The red bumper scores 10,000 or 100,000 when lit, and is lit by pressing the On Bumpers button just below the top saucers. Lighting the red bumpers also lights the saucers just below the 10 and 11 lanes to score 500,000 points instead of 50,000, which can be a reasonable strategy on its own if you are only concerned with points and not free games. The red bumper and saucers are turned off by the Off Bumpers button near the flippers.

There are no in or out lanes. Slingshots, which always score 10,000, extend from the flippers to the edges of the table. The flippers are quite far apart, with three out lanes between them, which score 100,000 points each and the A, B, or C. The posts that separate these lanes are approximately the same size as more flippers, giving adequate room to nudge a ball back into play if it is out of reach of the actual flippers themselves. The only way to drain a ball other than down the center out lanes is the gobble hole in the center of the game.

There is no extra ball feature, and no end of ball bonus other than the 100,000 for a center out lane or the 500,000 and potential Specials from the gobble hole. Tilt ends game. Since all scoring is in multiples of 10,000 points (up to 8,990,000), the match feature is indicated by a lit star next to one of the multiples of 10,000 shown along the bottom of the game.
